CORE VOICE
Clear, warm, confident, specific.

Simple and clear
Make it easy to understand. Prefer plain words to jargon, and short sentences to long ones. Aim for a reading level a busy viewer can follow on one listen.
- Skip the buzzwords. Say what the product does, not what category it "disrupts".
- Explain like a good teacher: understand it deeply, then say it simply.

Conversational
Write the way a knowledgeable colleague talks, not the way a press release reads.
- Use "we" for the company and "you" for the viewer.
- Contractions are fine. Starting a sentence with "and" or "but" is fine.

Confident, not loud
State things plainly and back them up.
- Use active voice and declarative sentences. Don't hedge.
- Let outcomes and specifics carry the claim: a real number beats an adjective.
- No hype words: revolutionary, game-changing, seamless, best-in-class.

Human
Lead with the people affected — customers, employees, the viewer — before the technology.
- Name the problem the way the viewer would describe it.
- One idea per sentence; one message per section.

HOUSE STYLE
- Sentence case for headings and on-screen titles.
- Numbers: digits for 10 and above, and for all metrics (3x, 40%).
- No emoji. No exclamation marks unless the moment truly earns one.
- Spell out an acronym the first time it is spoken.

WATCHOUTS
- Never invent customers, metrics, quotes or awards. Use only what the brief or source material supplies.
- Don't promise what the product can't do today.
- Keep it inclusive: avoid idioms that don't travel across regions.

EXAMPLES
Good: "Your agents see the next best action the moment a customer calls."
Bad: "Leverage our best-in-class AI-powered platform to revolutionize engagement."
